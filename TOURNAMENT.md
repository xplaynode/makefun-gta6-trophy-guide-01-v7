# TOURNAMENT — how the Night Ledger shape was chosen, and what v7 changes

Fourteen candidate concepts were scored head-to-head against the judge's own criteria
(tracker and data first, roadmap and missables second, cited guidance third, polish last),
using the live board's top-10 study and the measured record of our own earlier entries:
v3 "Leonida Night Ledger" placed #184 of ~6,536 with 333/333 cited steps, 14 sources and
6 disputes, while the thicker, differently-shaped v5 casebook placed worse at #343 despite
passing authorship at 91.2%. v6, a clean re-authoring of the v3 shape, climbed to #98.
The lesson the tournament had to respect: shape and density beat novelty — thicken the
weak surfaces of the proven shape, never replace the shape.

## The candidates

1. **v3-shape re-authoring (dark night ledger, three-pane)** — mirror v3's design language,
   page structure and density; lift only the surfaces the leaders beat it on. **Winner.**
2. Light-cream casebook (the v5 family) — rejected: v5 already proved this shape scores
   worse for us, whatever its citation volume.
3. Single-page checklist with expandable rows — loses the three-pane glance the top 10 all
   share; the judge's sheet rewards several parts visible at once.
4. Map-first guide (Leonida map as the home screen) — pretty, but the tracker data is not
   checkable at a glance and no map artwork of our own reads as well as a list.
5. Spreadsheet-style dense table — maximal density, minimal polish; fails criterion 4.
6. Card deck / swipe tracker — hides most trophies behind interaction; screenshots suffer.
7. Print-style newspaper broadsheet — charming, but the audit strip and counters fight the
   metaphor and type shrinks below glanceable size.
8. Timeline-only roadmap app — strong on missables, weak on per-trophy guidance.
9. Quiz-driven planner ("answer questions, get a route") — indirect; the judge cannot see
   the data without playing along.
10. Terminal / CLI-styled ledger — distinctive, but monospace density hurts readability at
    the judge's reduced screenshot size.
11. Mobile-first single column — the bounty is judged on desktop Chrome at 1280x800.
12. Maximalist dashboard (every stat on one screen) — everything visible, nothing legible;
    the audit strip already carries the numbers in the winning shape.
13. Minimalist two-pane (list + guide, no rail) — loses NEXT UP and the per-guide source
    list, two surfaces the leaders all carry.
14. Faithful v3 clone with no changes — parity is the floor, not the entry: it would repeat
    v3's exact weaknesses (6 disputes, 14 sources, no per-trophy missable box).

## What v7 adds on top of v6

A paid critique of v6 against the live leaders named three gaps, and v7 closes exactly
those three, inside the same shape:

1. **Provenance line** — the audit strip on every view now carries a second row: distinct
   pages and sites cited, the read date, and the missable-risk tally (possibly missable /
   at risk / unconfirmed), each number computed from the entry's own data files.
2. **Leader-matching screenshots** — the Missables view is now a standalone chapter-window
   grid (watched trophies as rows, Chapters 1-5 and Post as columns, open windows marked,
   the closing cell of each window marked LAST in red) with the story-gates list underneath,
   so the judged screen set is tracker, roadmap, missables grid, sources — like the leaders.
3. **Disputes 26 to 40** — fourteen more real disagreements mined from the same 16 sources
   (counts, session and time-estimate splits), each shown both ways with citations and a
   one-line "Our call: ... because ...", in the per-trophy SOURCES DIFFER boxes and in a
   register table with its own Our call column.
